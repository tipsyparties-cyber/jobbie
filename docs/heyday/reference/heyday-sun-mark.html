The Heyday sun

Four double-ended arrows make eight rays around a core, always in one colour: sunshine, and work flowing both ways. Its colour suits the section it sits in, and it's never black or orange. The same eight arrows bend, split and fold into a shape for every part of the business, so every section of the site has its own mark and they're all obviously one family.

It always turns back into the sun between shapes, so people see where each one comes from. Press any shape to try it, or leave it playing.

How the shapes work

Always the same eight arrows
Nothing is ever added. Arrows bend into waves or arcs, move apart, shorten into ticks, or tuck into the core.
One colour, always
Like your festival poster: the sun and every shape is a single colour. The sun takes the colour of wherever it sits, and it's never black or orange.
Arrowheads stay where they can
Any stroke that's still a ray keeps its arrowhead, so even the waves and the ring still feel like Heyday.
Back through the sun
Shape, sun, next shape. The mark is always the parent.

{ more info }

Tap "How it fits together" and the shape grows into the page

Each section shows its own shape in its own colour. Now and then it turns back into the sun and bounces. Tap the button and the shape grows from its core until it fills the section. The core swells as it grows so the gaps close, and the section becomes that colour, with the detail for that one thing. "Back" shrinks it home.

{ for the build }

The numbers, for Claude Code

  • Drawing: a 100-unit square. The sun is four lines through the centre at 0°, 45°, 90° and 135°, tips 40 from the centre, 8 thick, with round ends. Each tip has an 11-long arrowhead at ±40°. The core has a radius of 10, in the same colour as the rays.
  • The sun's colour: always one colour, and never black or orange.
    • In a group's section, it takes that group's colour. Where the group colour is ink or orange (Run the day, Get ahead), it's blue instead.
    • On plain grounds it's blue #93B7E8 on cream and paper, paper on blue, sage and sky, and sky on ink.
    • When it turns into a shape, its colour blends into the shape's.
  • Shapes: every shape is the same eight strokes (17 points each) and one core. The exact points for all ten shapes are in the motion file below, with the small engine that morphs between them.
  • Morph: every point moves to its place in the next shape in 0.9s with cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1), and the colour blends at the same time. Always go through the sun between two shapes. The Loop spins at 60° a second and the Infinity flows while they're showing.
  • Bounce: 0.7s, a hop up 12%, then a squash on landing (1.08 wide, 0.92 tall), then it settles. The dial click is 45° in 0.7s with a small overshoot.
  • Growing into the page:
    • The shape scales up around its own core. The final scale is the distance to the section's farthest corner, divided by 46% of the shape's width, plus 8%.
    • It takes 0.9s, and starts slowly then speeds up.
    • As it grows, the core swells to a radius of 46, so the gaps close.
    • Then the detail layer, in the same colour, switches on underneath, and its content rises in over 0.45s.
    • "Back" fades the content, then shrinks the shape home in 0.7s.
    • Esc closes it too. Focus goes to "Back" when it opens and returns to the button when it closes.
  • Reduced motion and off screen: the shapes show finished and still, and "more info" simply swaps.
