The big idea: one workflow for the whole business

HoneyBook and Jobber both say “all-in-one”. What they leave out is the team who deliver, their shifts and pay, classes alongside services, the aftercare and the marketplace. So Heyday's pitch is the whole workflow, not just the paperwork: “One workflow for your whole business, from the first hello to the next booking.”

Hero idea for jobbie: the workflow, rising

After anyone.com's hero, in our own art. Tall white cards rise one at a time through a panel on the right, each one a step of a real job. As a card lands, the Heyday sun peeks in, bounces and nudges it, an orange ripple spreads, and the card's details pop in. It's fun and positive, it plays on the “day” in the name, and it shows the whole workflow without a word. This is a still frame.

  • Cards: 300×420, white, 16px corners, 2px ink outline, hard shadow 11px 11px 0 at 8% ink.
  • Steps: new enquiry → instant quote → booked → deposit paid → team confirmed → the day → new review → rebooked, then loop.
  • Timing (measured from anyone.com): each card rises about 680px over 1.67s, holds for 0.83s, then leaves upward over 2.5s, with cubic-bezier(0.333, 0, 0.667, 1). A new card starts every 3s, so eight steps loop every 24s. Details pop in over 1s with cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.18, 1).
  • Where it shows: screens 1280px and wider. Narrower screens show the landed card still. With reduced motion, the loop stops on one card.
  • heyday.co uses the same motion with activities and hosts on the cards (a pottery night, a cocktail class at yours, a supper club) and photo slots.

“Build it your way”: the workflow builder

A section on jobbie that shows owners dragging steps into their workflow, then clicking any step to customise it. Eventually a short demo video; for now this still stands in for it. The customise panel is the settings sentence from the brief: when, wait, send, by, to, unless.

The rules that keep them cohesive

  • One gridEvery icon is drawn on a 48-unit square, with 4 units of breathing room.
  • One strokeA 3.2-unit ink stroke with round ends and joins, echoing the wordmark's soft weight.
  • One accentExactly one orange shape per icon, marking what the feature does for you.
  • No outlines inside fillsAccent shapes are solid, so they still read at small sizes.
  • Soft cornersRadii of 2 to 7 units; nothing sharp, nothing bubbly.
  • Same in both themesOn dark grounds, swap ink for cream and keep the orange.

The cards on this page use the outlined, offset-shadow style you liked from The Magic 8, to show how it sits with the icons. The values are measured from that site: a 1px ink outline, 40px corners and a hard 6px 10px shadow at 8% ink, with no blur.
