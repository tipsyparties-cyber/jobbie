# Hey Day stats bank: real statistics for the website, with sources

**Checked:** 13 September 2026.
**Method:** each figure was read on the publisher’s own page where possible. The full research notes, with exact wording and samples, are in the session’s working file. The rows below are the ones safe to use.

**Rules for using a statistic on the site:**
- **Show the source, the year and who it covers,** in small print under the number, and link it. The `StatBlock` component in the jobbie build brief enforces this.
- **Order of preference:**
  - Tipsy’s or Hey Day’s own figures, once Jem and Russell approve them
  - independent studies
  - a vendor’s own data, labelled as that vendor’s (“Flashquotes’ data from its customers”)
- **Don’t stretch a figure.** Say what the study measured, not more. For example, the Harvard study measured leads *qualified*, not bookings.
- **Say where a study is from another industry or country** (healthcare, restaurants, the UK) when it is.

## Don’t use these (widely quoted, no traceable source, or wrong)

- **“Quotes sent within 4 hours book 25% more often” credited to Jobber.** It’s Flashquotes’ figure (row 1), not Jobber’s.
- **Jobber’s “get paid 4x faster”, “80% of campaigns lead to new work” (70% on another page) and “90% say reviews influence their decision”.** None has a source, and two contradict each other.
- **Jobber’s “Marketing Tools customers grow 2x faster”.** It compares users with non-users, so it shows correlation, not cause.
- **“85% of callers won’t call back” and “62% call a competitor instead”.** They appear only on AI-receptionist sellers’ pages, with no study behind them.
- **“Fewer than 3% of callers leave a voicemail”.** Found only in secondary copies.
- **“67% prefer to book online (GetApp 2024)”.** The original can’t be found.
- **“Managers spend 8+ hours a week on scheduling (Deputy)”.** The original can’t be found.
- **“Good, better, best raises the average ticket 15 to 25%”.** Vendor blogs with no source.
- **Nielsen 2015 “83% trust recommendations”.** The page is gone; use the 2012 figure (row 9).
- **“68% of gift card users spend more than the value”.** It conflicts with the same body’s 61% (row 22).
- **“Time spent chasing late payments”, credited to QuickBooks.** It isn’t in either report.
